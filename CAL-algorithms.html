Projection optimization for computed axial lithography

In computed axial lithography, a rotating vial of photoresin is illuminated by a sequence of computed images, and the object forms wherever the accumulated light dose crosses the material’s gelation threshold. Choosing those images is an inverse problem: light can only be added, never subtracted, and the material responds nonlinearly to dose. Straightforward filtered back-projection therefore produces poor parts, and the quality of a print is set as much by the optimization as by the hardware.

We have developed a sequence of optimization methods for this problem. Early work introduced high-fidelity projection generation that accounts for the material response. In collaboration with the University of Colorado and Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory we developed Object Space Model Optimization (OSMO). Most recently, a band-constrained Lp-norm (BCLP) formulation treats the target as a tolerance band rather than a binary image. That generalization handles grayscale responses, spatially varying tolerances and weights, and places earlier algorithms within a common framework. Current work addresses the co-optimization of optical and diffusive blur, and scale-invariant formulations for large prints.
